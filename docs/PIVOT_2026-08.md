# PIVOT — back to the prediction engine

*Decided 2026-08-08. This document supersedes [VISION_2030.md](VISION_2030.md)
and the roadmap derived from it. VISION_2030 stays in the repo as a record of
what was attempted and why it was stopped; nothing in it is active work.*

---

## 1. Why we are pivoting

The project started on 2025-09-22 as **"soccer_predictor — An AI/ML based
prediction tool."** On 2026-07-14 VISION_2030 reframed it as "a world model of
football" with seven flagship concepts and four verbs. Most of those shipped:
Counterfactual Machine, Rarity Engine, Story Compiler, Boardroom, Almanac,
momentum river, match2vec, a 3D Match Theater that was built, reverted, and now
sits as tested dead code mounted on zero pages.

The repo today is 131,642 lines, 26 pages, 67 API routes, 9 workflows.

Underneath all of it, the prediction engine — the actual product — went
sideways for nine consecutive weeks.

### 1.1 The measurement that forced the decision

Scored over all 1,530 settled picks in `backend/data/predictions/`, against
baselines computed on the identical match set:

Scored over all 1,530 settled picks in `backend/data/predictions/`:

| | accuracy | Brier | log loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Model, as published** (`predicted_winner`) | .4359 | — | — |
| **Model, argmax of its own probabilities** | .4627 | **.6324** | 1.0502 |
| Constant global base rate | .4562 | **.6414** | 1.0615 |
| Uniform ⅓ | .4562 | .6667 | 1.0986 |

A constant that knows nothing about any match scores .6414 Brier. Sixty-six
features, seven-model per-league ensembles, ELO, xG, weather and referee
factors buy **0.009**.

### 1.2 The real target, measured on our own data

`backend/scripts/benchmark_market.py` scored the closing line over **25,746
settled fixtures with odds**, 7 leagues, 2015-08 → 2026-03. This replaces the
literature estimate — it is the actual bar:

| forecaster | Brier | log loss | RPS | accuracy | ECE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Market (Shin de-vig)** | **.5666** | .9552 | .1921 | **.5456** | **.0049** |
| Market (proportional) | .5668 | .9556 | .1921 | .5456 | .0068 |
| Constant base rate | .6468 | 1.0693 | .2303 | .4427 | — |
| Uniform ⅓ | .6667 | 1.0986 | .2366 | .4427 | — |

Per league the close ranges from **.539** (Primeira Liga) to **.587**
(Ligue 1), and is stable across all 11 seasons (.552–.579). The market's own
ECE of .0049 is independent evidence the de-vigging is right — closing odds are
very nearly perfectly calibrated.

Paired head-to-head on the 786 fixtures where a model prediction and closing
odds both exist:

| forecaster | Brier | log loss | RPS | accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Model | .6396 | 1.0601 | .2211 | .4427 |
| **Market (Shin)** | **.5797** | .9747 | .1950 | **.5496** |
| Constant base rate | .6516 | 1.0765 | .2297 | .4288 |

**Gap: +.0599 Brier, +.0854 log loss, −10.7 accuracy points.** The market beats
the constant by .0719; the model beats it by .0120. The engine captures
**≈17% of the distance** from "knows nothing" to the closing line. Not one of
the seven leagues is within noise of the close — the smallest gap is the
Premier League at +.0212 (n=157), the largest Primeira Liga at +.0952 (n=93).

Coverage is stated honestly: 1,530 settled → 1,510 in scope → 1,016 joined a
warehouse fixture → 786 had odds. The shortfall is genuine data absence, not
matching failure — football-data.co.uk's current-season files stop around
2026-03-03, and MLS 2026, NWSL and World Cup are not in the odds corpus at all.

### 1.2 Corroborating evidence

- **Walk-forward is worse than uniform.** `diagnostics/walkforward_*.json`:
  Brier .70–.84, log loss 1.22–1.50, ECE .25–.34. Uniform ⅓ is .667 / 1.099.
- **The advertised number does not survive contact with reality.** CLAUDE.md
  claims 60.56% holdout accuracy for `unified_men`. Live is 46%. A 14-point
  holdout-to-live gap is a temporal-leakage signature in the split, not noise.
- **Nine weeks of ignored regression.** `training_drift_*.json` reported
  `"status": "regression"` every week from 2026-06-07 onward. For the first
  eight the gate recorded the regression and promoted all ten models anyway
  (`n_held_back: 0`). On 2026-08-02 it finally held back three and failed the
  job. The CI "failure" was the guardrail working for the first time.
- **The decision policy was destroying accuracy.** A tuned draw gate overrode
  argmax on 355 of 1,530 picks and was right on 94 of them where argmax got
  135. It skewed the pick distribution to 395 draws where argmax says 116.
  Removed 2026-08-08 in `backend/main.py` and
  `backend/scripts/predict_upcoming.py`; worth +2.7 accuracy points for free.

## 2. What this project is now

> **A professional soccer dashboard built on a prediction model that is as
> accurate as the state of the art allows, measured against the only honest
> yardstick there is — the market — and extended across the season into title,
> relegation and final-table projections.**

Three deliverables, nothing else:

1. **Match outcome prediction.** 1X2 and scoreline, calibrated, scored against
   closing odds.
2. **Season projections.** As each matchday resolves: who wins the league, who
   goes down, what the final table looks like.
3. **A value surface.** Model probability vs no-vig implied probability, with
   expected value and Kelly staking exposed as real math.

If a feature is not one of those three, it does not belong here.

### 2.1 The betting decision

The educational-only constraint pinned in README, CLAUDE.md and ROADMAP is
**retired**. The product surfaces value bets: no-vig implied probabilities,
+EV flags, Kelly-fraction staking.

This raises the bar rather than lowering it. A dashboard that recommends a bet
is only defensible if it can show it beats the closing line, so the market
benchmark in §3 is not optional infrastructure — it is the licence to ship the
feature at all. Until the model demonstrably closes the gap on the market on a
league, that league ships **no** value flags. Calibration gating, RaceIQ-style,
is the mechanism.

Responsible-gambling framing, jurisdictional notice, and stake limits ship with
the surface, not after it.

## 3. The market benchmark is the scoreboard

Everything hangs off this. The warehouse schema already carries
`odds_home`, `odds_draw`, `odds_away`, `odds_over_2_5` from football-data.co.uk
(`backend/services/data/warehouse.py`), but `backend/data/warehouse.sqlite` is
gitignored and currently 0 bytes locally. Rebuilding it with odds is the first
engineering task.

The harness must produce, per league per season:

- **No-vig implied probabilities** from closing odds (proportional and
  Shin methods; report both).
- **Model vs market** on Brier, log loss, and RPS, paired on identical fixtures.
- **Closing-line value** — the only measure that predicts betting profitability.
- **Calibration curves and ECE**, per league, per probability bucket.

A model is "state of the art" for a league when it is within noise of the
closing line on that league's paired Brier. Nothing else counts as evidence.

**Baselines that must be beaten, in order:** constant base rate → home-advantage
Elo → Dixon-Coles → market. The current engine has not cleanly cleared the
first.

### 3.1 The Dixon-Coles floor, measured 2026-08-08

`backend/scripts/benchmark_dc_challenger.py` fits an **off-the-shelf
`penaltyblog` Dixon-Coles** — default `xi=0.0018`, no tuning, no features
beyond goals and time decay — walk-forward across 12,289 Wave A fixtures,
training only on seasons strictly before each test season:

| league | n | DC Brier | market Brier | DC acc | market acc |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Premier League | 2614 | .5871 | .5658 | .5256 | .5513 |
| Serie A | 2565 | .5905 | .5701 | .5287 | .5435 |
| La Liga | 2670 | .5956 | .5814 | .5161 | .5307 |
| Bundesliga | 2026 | .6003 | .5798 | .5000 | .5281 |
| Ligue 1 | 2414 | .6170 | .5893 | .4942 | .5195 |
| **ALL** | **12289** | **.5977** | **.5770** | **.5138** | **.5351** |

Gap to market, the fixture-set-independent comparison:

| model | Brier gap to close |
|---|---|
| In-house neural ensemble | **+.0599** |
| Off-the-shelf Dixon-Coles | **+.0207** |

**A model with no referee, weather, h2h, xG or form features — three hundred
lines of textbook statistics — captures roughly 70% of the market's signal
where the bespoke 66-feature neural stack captures 17%.**

Consequences for the plan:

1. **Dixon-Coles is now the serving default for Wave A**, not a fallback. The
   neural stack does not serve a prediction in a league until it beats DC
   out-of-sample in that league.
2. Feature work (§4b) should be evaluated as *increments on the DC floor*, not
   on the neural baseline. A feature that cannot improve a well-specified
   statistical model is unlikely to be rescuing a neural one.
3. The neural ensemble is on probation. If it cannot clear the floor after the
   data-integrity fixes in §4c and the ablation in §4b, it should be deleted
   rather than carried.
4. `penaltyblog` also ships bivariate Poisson, zero-inflated Poisson, Weibull
   copula and hierarchical Bayesian goal models plus Elo/Massey/Colley/Pi
   ratings. Those are the next challengers to run against the same harness —
   the hierarchical Bayesian model in particular pools strength across teams,
   which is exactly what thin-data leagues need.

Caveats: DC and the neural ensemble were scored on different fixture sets
(12,289 vs 786), so only the gap-to-market column is a fair head-to-head. DC
skips fixtures with a team unseen in training. Wave A men's only.

## 4. Season projections need to exist as a measured thing

`backend/services/simulation/league_simulator.py` already emits
`title_probability`, `relegation_probability`, `avg_final_position`. There is
**no backtest, no calibration, no diagnostics artifact** for any of it —
`diagnostics/` holds `walkforward_*.json` for match prediction and nothing at
all for standings. The user has now made this a first-class goal, and it has
never been measured.

Required before it ships:

- Replay every completed season in the warehouse matchday by matchday.
- At each matchday, record projected title/relegation/final-position
  distributions.
- Score against what actually happened: Brier on title and relegation, MAE on
  final position, and a calibration curve on the projection buckets.
- Publish as `diagnostics/season_projection_*.json`, on the same footing as the
  match walk-forward.

The interesting output is the **convergence curve** — how many matchdays before
the projection is trustworthy. That is a genuinely good dashboard artifact and
nobody else ships it honestly.

## 4b. Features: every edge, but each one earning its place

The goal is to exploit every signal that helps — head-to-head history, the
specific stadium and each team's record at it, referee tendencies and
referee×team interactions, weather, kickoff time, congestion, travel.

One caution has to be stated up front, because it changes how this work is
done. `features.py` already carries 40 features and the unified model ~66,
including five h2h features, home/away venue splits, rest days, derby and
match-importance flags. With all of that the model captures 0.009 Brier over a
constant. The bottleneck is therefore very unlikely to be a shortage of
features, and adding unvalidated ones to a model that cannot extract signal
from 66 adds variance rather than accuracy. `features.py` currently includes
`home_news_sentiment` / `away_news_sentiment` / `home_news_factor` /
`away_news_factor`, which are prime suspects for pure noise.

So the rule is: **a feature ships only when an ablation shows it improves
out-of-sample Brier.** Concretely:

- Candidate features are built in `features_v2.py` and proven before they touch
  the production feature set.
- `backend/scripts/ablate_features.py` evaluates baseline, baseline + each
  candidate group, and greedy forward selection, writing
  `diagnostics/feature_ablation.json`.
- Splits are **strictly temporal** — train before a cutoff, test after. Never
  random k-fold. Random folds over football fixtures are the most likely
  explanation for 60.56% holdout against 46% live.
- Every feature is computed from information available **before kickoff only**.
  Point-in-time correctness is enforced structurally and tested, not assumed.
- Ablation results are reported against the market row, not in isolation.
- Features that make things worse are removed, including existing ones.

Known gaps to close first, since they are data problems rather than modelling
ones: referee and weather are currently applied as post-hoc multipliers
(`referee_factor`, `weather_factor`) rather than trained inputs; venue coverage
is 9,153 of 32,569 matches and attendance 5,174; xG needs the Understat/FBref
loaders. Sparse coverage must be handled as genuine missingness, never
imputed with a fabricated value.

## 4a. THE BUG — train/serve market-feature skew

Found 2026-08-08 by the feature ablation. This single defect explains the
60.56%-holdout / 46%-live gap. The chronological split was never the problem.

`feature_builder_v2.FEATURE_NAMES` (lines 84–85) contains five market features
built from `matches.odds_*` — `implied_home_prob`, `implied_draw_prob`,
`implied_away_prob`, `implied_over_2_5`, `market_overround` — plus an
interaction term `implied_home_x_form` at line 743. Those columns are populated
for **96.1% of Wave A training rows**, so the model learns to lean on them.

The serving path then sets them to zero. `unified_inference.py:236-237`
synthesises the match row with literal `NULL AS odds_home, NULL AS odds_draw,
NULL AS odds_away, NULL AS odds_over_2_5`, and `feature_builder_v2.py:823-827`
turns absent odds into `0.0`.

Measured over 8,664 out-of-sample Wave A fixtures:

| | Brier | log loss | accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|
| trained with odds, served **with** odds | **.5801** | .9752 | **.5308** |
| trained with odds, served with **zeros** | **.6561** | 1.0853 | **.4358** |

A 9.5-point accuracy collapse, landing *below* the constant base rate (.6497) —
precisely where the live track record sits.

**Fix, and it is a fork, not a detail.** Per §3.1's two-model split:

- **Model A (dashboard, accuracy-optimised)** keeps the market features and
  gets real odds plumbed into the inference path. Expect ≈.58 Brier.
- **Model B (value surface)** must drop them entirely and retrain. A model
  that reads the closing line agrees with it by construction and can never
  find a +EV bet.

Serving the features as zeros is strictly worse than either choice. Nothing
else in this document matters until this is resolved.

## 4c. Data integrity — found and FIXED 2026-08-08

All nine integrity checks now pass (`backend/scripts/validate_warehouse_integrity.py`,
which exits non-zero on violation and reports 2/9 against the pre-repair backup —
it is a real guard, not a rubber stamp).

**The root cause was one bug, and it was not the one first suspected.** The
initial hypothesis — ESPN and football-data disagreeing on season labels — was
wrong. They agree. What actually happened: football-data's terse club names
("Swansea", "Ath Madrid", "Sp Lisbon") scored 0.55–0.84 against the ESPN
spelling, below `team_resolver`'s 0.92 auto-merge threshold, so a **second
`teams` row** was created. `_find_existing_match` keys on team ids, so it then
failed to see the ESPN fixture and **inserted a duplicate**. That single defect
inflated team counts *and* row counts, and 53 of the 55 suspect league-seasons
were this. Only 2 were a genuine season-label bug (Serie A 2019-20's
COVID-delayed final matchday, played 1 Aug 2020, filed as 2020-21).

Concretely: Dortmund "won" the 2018-19 Bundesliga in our warehouse because its
7-0 over Nürnberg was counted twice — +3 points and +7 goal difference. The
season now reconstructs Bayern 78, Dortmund 76, at exactly 306 fixtures, which
is reality.

Once identities merged, the duplicate count turned out to be **15× the original
estimate**: 1,278 groups, not 82. 1,279 rows removed, with 1,988 fields
coalesced into the survivors so no odds, referee or shot data was lost.

**A second one-line bug** corrupted every kickoff time. The host timezone is
`Asia/Jerusalem`; the loader built a *naive* local-midnight datetime from
football-data's date-only column and called `.astimezone(utc)` on it, which
Python interprets as host-local — shifting every row back across midnight.
Day-of-week was wrong for 86% of Wave A. Proof of diagnosis: reinterpreting all
23,416 fdcouk rows through `Asia/Jerusalem` landed every single one on exact
midnight. Fixed with `.replace(tzinfo=utc)`.

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| xG | 0 | **19,296 (100%)** |
| weather rows | 0 | **12,850** |
| clubs with venue lat/lon | 0 | **163 (100%)** |

### Still open, honestly

- **Referees outside England are a source limitation, not a parsing bug.**
  football-data publishes a `Referee` column for England only (plus Serie A
  2005–07). ESPN carries officials but only on the per-match `summary`
  endpoint and only from 2022-23; earlier seasons return `officials: null`.
  esp/ger/ita/fra sit at 0.8–1.8%. Full coverage needs resolving 13,687 rows
  to ESPN event ids — a much larger scrape, not yet attempted. **Referee
  features remain untestable for four of five Wave A leagues.**
- **Kickoff times before 2019** do not exist in the source (`Time` starts
  2019-20). 2019–2025 are 100%; 2015–2018 stay at 00:00:00Z meaning "date
  known, kickoff unknown". Weather is written only for the 12,503 matches with
  a real kickoff — conditions at an unknown hour are not a measurement.
- **`feature_builder_v2` still hardcodes** the weather defaults (15.0°C etc.)
  and `away_travel_km = 0.0`. Both are now computable — travel for 100% of
  Wave A via `venue_loader.haversine_km` (median 310 km, max 2,260 km). Wiring
  them is the next modelling task, followed by re-running the ablation.

## 5. League scope, in order

Depth first. Expand only on measured evidence.

| Wave | Competitions | Gate to advance |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Premier League, La Liga, Bundesliga, Ligue 1, Serie A | Paired Brier within noise of closing line; season projections calibrated |
| **B** | MLS | Wave A holding for a full season |
| **C** | Champions League, Europa League, Euros, World Cup, Copa América | Wave B holding; tournament-specific projection backtest |

Everything currently served outside Wave A — women's competitions, Eredivisie,
Primeira Liga, and the rest — comes **out of the product** until its wave
arrives. The thin-data competitions are a large part of what drags the
aggregate calibration toward the base rate, and shipping them dishonestly is
what the pivot is correcting.

This is a scope reduction with a real cost: the women's-game parity commitment
in VISION_2030 §9 is being dropped for now. That was a good commitment and it
should return in a later wave, on the same evidence gate as everything else,
rather than being quietly carried as an unmeasured claim.

## 6. Frontend — simpler, professional, RaceIQ's language

The reference is the sibling project `f1_predictions` (RaceIQ), whose
"Bugatti" design language is deliberately austere:

- Pure black canvas `#000000`; surfaces at `#0d0d0d` / `#141414`; hairline
  borders at `#262626`. No gradients, no shadows, no glassmorphism, no chrome.
- White uppercase letterspaced display type, serif body, monospace for nav,
  buttons and captions.
- No accent color as decoration. Chromatic signal is reserved for meaning —
  live state, positive/negative, positional tiers.
- Photography is the only depth element.

RaceIQ ships **7 pages**. Pitchverse ships **26**. The target is parity with
RaceIQ:

| Page | Content |
|---|---|
| Home | Next matchday predictions across Wave A leagues |
| Match detail | Per-match forecast, probabilities, market comparison |
| Standings | Live table + projected final table |
| Title & relegation | Monte Carlo season projections with convergence curve |
| Accuracy | Track record vs market and vs baselines, calibration curves |
| Fixtures | Schedule |
| About | Methodology, model card, responsible-gambling notice |

RaceIQ has already solved the two hardest presentational problems here —
calibration gating with an explicit "Calibrated" badge, and grading its own
predictions against a named baseline round by round. Both port directly.

## 8. Sequence

**Phase 1 — stop the bleeding.** Draw gate removed ✅. Fix the training gate so
a regression blocks promotion instead of recording it. Fix the warehouse
refresh timeout. Rebuild the warehouse with odds.

**Phase 2 — the scoreboard.** Market benchmark harness (§3). Re-score every
historical prediction against closing odds. Publish the honest number, whatever
it is. Investigate the 60.56%-vs-46% holdout gap as a leakage bug.

**Phase 3 — earn the accuracy back.** Wave A only. Dixon-Coles as the floor.
Nothing serves a prediction until it beats that floor out-of-sample.

**Phase 4 — season projections.** Backtest the simulator (§4). Ship title and
relegation with a convergence curve.

**Phase 5 — the frontend.** Rebuild on the Bugatti language, 26 pages → 7.

**Phase 6 — the value surface.** EV and Kelly, gated per league on Phase 2
evidence. No league ships flags until it earns them.

## 9. The standing rules, revised

Replacing the VISION_2030 §9 constraints:

- **The market is the benchmark.** Any accuracy claim is stated as paired
  Brier/log-loss against closing odds on named fixtures, or it is not stated.
- **Calibration gates the product.** A league with no evidence ships no value
  flags. Confidence displayed never exceeds confidence measured.
- **Baselines are never deleted.** Constant, Elo and Dixon-Coles stay live as
  the yardstick. A model that cannot beat them does not serve.
- **A regression blocks promotion.** No more recording a regression and
  shipping anyway.
- **No fabricated data.** Carried forward from the old rules unchanged.
- **Scope discipline.** Match outcomes, season projections, value surface. A
  feature that is none of the three does not get built.
